THE CHALLENGE

A new brand doesn't win on price. It wins on trust.

When a distributor adds a brand, the dentist already has a trusted one in the operatory. If the newcomer's marketing looks improvised, it starts at a disadvantage: nobody swaps a material that works for one that looks generic.

A dental brand's first impression doesn't come from the product. It comes from how the brand shows up.

So the launch started with a system, not one-off posts: the triangles on DiaDent's packaging turned into hero elements, one color per product, headlines that work as brand ideas — "Clean canals. Free-moving files." —, product photos I shot myself for several of the pieces, and technical data checked against the manufacturer's information, shown as a spec sheet, never as a sale sticker.
